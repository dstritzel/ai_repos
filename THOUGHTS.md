## Final Thoughts

### Do I like AI?

I do not hate AI; I dislike the buzzword and the poorly executed usage by some companies. Governments need to get reports from companies on how many people they replace with AI. If they have to rehire workers, they should be penalized by contributing to unemployment and Social Security pools. Already contributing to those pools? Then they should contribute more. Many wanted the quick buck and started replacing people with AI even though the tech and models are decades from fully replacing human roles. This creates a social stigma that is hurting people.

### Datacenters

Under no circumstances should the community/city/etc. be required to contribute to the power usage of a datacenter, especially an AI datacenter. "It will create jobs" is actually a fallacy if the AI datacenter uses AI for those jobs it creates. These mega datacenters are not desirable. Space datacenters might be feasible, but there are likely many ecological and latency issues. Not sure if solar panels will be enough. Ideally, we should get the tech to use less power, generate less heat, and use less space.

### Ethical uses

A business eithics for AI use needs to be planned and implemented, kind of like the securities for insider trading, ect. A limitation on company profits vs AI usage needs to be in place. THe problem is not always the company itself but the shareholders wanting a massive quick profit. I think company investments should be a decade or decades plan and not quarterly/yearly, but capitalistic greed makes it bad. Layoffs and job market issues are caused by this. At least partially. All companies after a certain size should be able to maintain their workers for several years on stored income. The larger the profit, the bigger the buffer. If you lay someone off because of AI or profit problems you should be required to contribute to the pension or larger severance. Masking layoffs by fake HR reports on performance should be prison time.

Sorry went from AI to modern business practices, but AI in business use needs to be regulated to prevent a strain on social systems in any country. Social Security in the US and other ones in other countries.

### What I would like to see with AI.

#### Games

Large scale online games controlled by AI that is ever evolving. A mix of human and AI voice actors because the world is just too big. Big is subjective with Studio, Publishers, ect and yet to be defined.
Take Skyrim for example. Record human voice lines for the main quests, but have an AI expand upon it with the VA's permission, but only for that game and the same character in future games. But only as flavor text. For example the VA doesn't need to say "I stepped in a bucket of AI slop" a dozen different ways, they just need to focus on the real conversations with inflections.

#### Narrations

An AI that can interpret the feeling of the character in a book for audio narration and use it. Also having a different voice and accent per character. Imagine the author saying "John the dragon slayer" has a scottish accent and a deep voice, while "Sam the baguette burgler" has an english accent with a whispy voice. The narrator voice will stop reading and the character will say the words. <Sam Voice - Contemplative>"This baguette looks delicious!", <Narrator Voice - plain?> Sam says to himself and then grabs the baguette with a quiet, <Sam Voice - quiet> "Yoink!"

Let the author set the voices for each character.

In theory is sounds interesting, but in practive it chould be different. The reason the voices make a difference with multiple narrators is that you cannot be sure if it is Sam or John speaking if someone is just reading the book. Consider looking into Full Cast Audio or Soundbooth Theater. If AI cannot make it then someone can make edits to the text using SSML to have it done.
